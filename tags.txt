Tag colour reference
Every tag family in use across the platform, with its values and hexes. New values are held at 4.5:1 or better on their own fill. The inherited funding-stage ramp in section 1 is the exception — seven of its light tones sit between 2.65:1 and 4.43:1 and are grandfathered, marked △ in the table. Mappings are fixed platform-wide — do not re-map per page.

9 · Numeric value
Enterprise value, deal size, revenue — any figure shown as a tag rather than a table number. One form, one colour: brand blue on white with a blue hairline. Figures are data, not categories, so they never take a semantic hue and never carry a dot.

10 · Entity tags
Companies, investors, advisors, individuals, sectors and subsectors, wherever a set of linked entities is listed — report and news pages, profile cross-links, benchmark criteria. These are links, not classifications, so they take the .lp-chip geometry the product already uses for these chipsets — 999px pill, 24px tall, 12px weight 600 — with the borderless treatment .lp-chip-purple already uses, and never a dot. Absence of a dot is what separates an entity from the semantic chip families above, which frees the five hues to be picked purely for telling entity kinds apart.

Red belongs to News alone. With Divested removed from Transaction status, no status or stage value uses the red family — keep it that way, so a red pill always means "this is a news article".

Blue appears three times — the two Anticipated statuses, Fundraise in News subtypes, and Asymmetrix Assessment. They are separated by form, not hue: status is a dotted pill with no border, News subtypes are dotted and bordered, Transaction signal is a sentence-case 6px-radius tag with no dot. Keep those forms fixed and the blues stay readable together.

Entity tags share both hue and geometry with the semantic chip families — a green company chip and a Reported status chip differ only in that the status chip carries a dot. Never give an entity tag a dot, and never drop the dot from a status chip; that single rule carries the distinction.

Process stage and Transaction status share no hue — status holds green, amber, blue and neutral; process stage holds cyan, teal, violet, orange and indigo. They sit in adjacent Deal Radar columns, so keep that separation whenever either list gains a value.

Buyer type deliberately re-uses section 1’s hues — grey, purple, green mean corporate, private equity and growth capital in both places, which is the point. Cyan appears in Process stage (Strategic Review), Intermediary (named advisor) and I&A (Investor Analysis); the first is a filled dotted pill, the second carries a link, the third is a no-dot research pill. Green, amber and blue likewise carry meaning in more than one family — the platform pattern is one status tag plus one type tag per row, and the label carries the meaning when a row has to show both.
